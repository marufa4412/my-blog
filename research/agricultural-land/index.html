Agricultural Land Constraints in Asian Economies

Comparing consumption-based agricultural land demand with measured domestic biocapacity across 14 Asian economies, 2000–2022.

01 / Research question

When demand grows, does measured capacity keep pace?

Aggregate agricultural output alone cannot explain whether consumption-driven land demand is rising faster than the measured regenerative capacity assigned to an economy. This study examines the gap between these two accounting measures, rather than treating them as interchangeable indicators.

Why the question matters

Similar aggregate gaps may reflect different combinations of demand growth and capacity change. Cropland and grazing-land components also behave differently, which can affect the interpretation of resource pressure.

Research approach

Separate agricultural footprint (consumption-based demand) from agricultural biocapacity (measured supply capacity), examine changes over time and estimate associations with economic development using panel-data techniques.

02 / Data & methodology

Longitudinal comparison with econometric checks

01 · DEFINE

Construct measures

Use agricultural footprint, measured biocapacity and their ratio, with separate consideration of cropland and grazing components.

02 · ESTIMATE

Panel modeling

Apply two-way fixed effects to study within-economy associations while accounting for common year effects.

03 · ASSESS

Robust inference

Use Driscoll–Kraay standard errors, wild cluster bootstrap procedures and decomposition analysis as described in the report.

Data sources identified in the capstone report: National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (2024 edition), Our World in Data and FAOSTAT. Measurements are expressed in the accounting units used by the underlying sources.
